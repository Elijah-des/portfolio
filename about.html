Approach

Most of my work starts with noticing something people have quietly adapted to instead of questioning: a workaround, a small daily friction, a barrier so familiar it stopped registering as a problem at all. That's usually where the real design opportunity is: not in solving a problem someone can already articulate, but in surfacing one they've stopped seeing.

That means research and empathy work aren't a formality before the "real" design starts. They're where most of the actual thinking happens. I'd rather build a solution shaped by a specific person's specific constraint than something engineered to be broadly acceptable to everyone, because that specificity is usually what makes it genuinely work.

A lot of my strongest ideas come from carrying a strategy across contexts: something learned from one community or industry, applied to a completely different one. That cross-pollination tends to surface barriers and solutions neither side would have found alone, and it's the throughline across my practice, sharpened by a minor in Entrepreneurship & Social Innovation alongside my Industrial Design major.

Some of that thinking has already been tested in the real world. For The Dugout, a proposed conservation garden and community hub for Toronto's Quayside waterfront, I started with a site visit and conversations with locals about what they actually wanted from the space, then built that into a curated user interview survey and stakeholder maps to understand who the solution needed to serve. From there, the concept went through pitch presentations, iterations, and feedback forums before arriving at the final design.

How I work

Process

01

Research & empathy

Shadow, interview, and observe the people actually living with the problem before assuming what it is: the pain point is rarely the one people lead with.

02

Concept development

Generate a wide set of directions, then narrow using real constraints: material behavior, market gaps, what people actually said, not whichever idea looks most polished first.

03

Iteration & feedback

Build low-fidelity first, put it in front of the people it's for, and let their reactions, not just my own judgment, decide what changes next.

04

Refined outcome

Arrive at something specific to the actual constraint uncovered in step one, and stay honest in the writeup about what's still unresolved, rather than smoothing it over.
